Yağız Nalçakan, PhD

Research Professor at Yonsei University

I am a Research Professor at the Seamless Trans-X Lab (STL), Yonsei University, where I work with Prof. Shiho Kim on multispectral imaging and robust visual perception. My research sits at the intersection of computer vision, sensor fusion, and multimodal representation learning, with applications spanning intelligent mobility, robotics, and industrial and environmental sensing.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the Izmir Institute of Technology, where my dissertation focused on vehicle maneuver prediction for advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Yalin Bastanlar, in collaboration with TTTech Auto Turkey. During my doctoral studies, I spent a term as a visiting researcher at Seoul National University’s Vehicle Dynamics and Control Laboratory (VDCL) and the Future Mobility Technology Center (FMTC), supported by a research scholarship from TUBITAK.

My research interests center on computer vision and deep learning, with emphasis on multispectral camera systems spanning the visible, near-infrared, short-wave infrared, and long-wave infrared bands, perception under adverse conditions, representation learning, and vision-language modeling.

Research focus

  • Multispectral perception: Designing datasets and architectures that fuse RGB, NIR, SWIR, and LWIR imagery for detection, segmentation, and depth estimation.
  • Multimodal foundation models and representation learning: Extending self-supervised vision foundation models beyond RGB, and studying cross-spectral translation and domain adaptation under raw-sensor domain shift.
  • Vision-language and explainable perception: Exploring how multimodal models can interpret challenging scenes and make their reasoning inspectable.
